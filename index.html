Why these three

Not three prettier versions of what you have.

Your decorated cookies are photographed the way the whole category photographs them: one cookie, centred, on white. It is a good catalogue photograph. It is the wrong job.

A white cutout proves the shape and deletes everything else. It deletes the hand that piped that squirrel's tail this morning. It deletes how small the cookies are. It deletes the dog. Those are the only three things a factory cannot copy.

What the white background deletes.

The hand
Piped one at a time by named people — Shelly, Erin and Elizabeth are on your own About page. On white, a hand-piped squirrel tail and an injection-moulded one are indistinguishable.
The scale
A paw cookie on white could be two inches across or eight. The most-asked, least-answered question in the category, and nothing in it has a reference object.
The dog
Two hundred and ninety-two products for dogs, and almost no dogs.
The occasion
Fourteen birthday SKUs plus Gotcha Day, and not one photograph of a birthday. A $3.49 cookie is an impulse; a $29.99 cake is planned a week ahead. Two different pictures, currently getting the same one.

The exception, already yours

You have taken the right photograph once, by accident.

The hero image for Mystery Critters, $6.99 — two cupped hands holding four iced cookies over a lawn, shot from above in daylight. Scale, a person, colour. It is the best photograph on your website, and it has never been an ad.

So you do not need a photographer or a studio. You already own the picture that works. The job is to do it on purpose, three times.

Voice of customer

Nobody describes the taste.

Fourteen reviews on your own reviews page, and not one describes flavour, ingredients or health. They describe how the cookie looks. Customers buy this with their eyes, which makes photography the product experience rather than a supporting asset — and makes a white cutout an unusually expensive mistake for this brand specifically.

The products in these three images are generated, not photographed.

What differs, specifically

The green paw print on the squirrel has its pads merged. Yours is four round toes above one three-lobed pad.

The mint Mystery Critter is fully mint-iced. Yours is a tan biscuit with a mint swirl and dense nonpareils.

The Canine Cupcakes have a flatter frosting peak, and a pale biscuit rim where yours are iced to the edge.

The biscuit reads a shade warmer and more gingerbread than your darker carob.

That is inherent to this kind of work and cannot be prompted away. Better written down here than found later.

So these ship as concept creative — proof of a direction, not advertising to put spend behind tomorrow. Before any runs as a paid ad, one of two things:

  1. The hero product is photographed for real in the same setup, or
  2. You confirm the depiction is accurate to what a customer receives.

Your call, made with your eyes open. For a US advertiser it is an accuracy-of-depiction question, not a matter of taste.

Everything checked and everything rejected is in the QC log with the files. Three frames passed, none failed. Four versions of DB-03 were binned — three because the model kept putting a plastic balloon topper on a cake you do not sell.
